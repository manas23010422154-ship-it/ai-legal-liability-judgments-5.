01 · Overview

What this project examines

The project asks how the Court has responded when fabricated or allegedly AI-generated legal authorities appear in judicial proceedings, and what responsibility remains with the people who use or rely on those authorities.

04 · Methodology

How the cases were selected

The project report describes a focused search for matters that directly address AI-generated or fabricated authorities in adjudication.

1

Search terms

The report lists “Artificial Intelligence,” “AI-generated,” “hallucinated,” “fake judgments” and “synthetic judgments.”

2

Relevance screening

Include matters within the study period where the judicial record directly addresses AI-generated or fabricated authorities in adjudication. Do not add unrelated technology cases merely to increase the count.

3

Read and code

Record the date, case number, procedural status, issue, response, outcome and human-accountability themes. Check case details against the official court record.

The project report identifies published Supreme Court judgment/order texts as its source base. It does not provide a final search-result count for the complete database search, so this page does not invent one.

05 · Limitations

How to interpret these findings

  • The current dataset has only two coded matters and does not yet meet the assignment guide's requested 10–20 judgments.
  • The matters are procedurally different: one is a reasoned judgment with a substantive outcome; the other is an interim order. They should not be treated as equivalent precedents.
  • The results cannot establish the frequency of AI-related litigation, prove that no other relevant Supreme Court matters exist, or settle general civil or criminal liability for autonomous AI systems.
  • Charts are descriptive counts of the current spreadsheet entries. They should be regenerated when the dataset is expanded or corrected.
